## Stage model

Canada does **not** use a single legislated stage ladder. Three native vocabularies coexist:

**1. TAC CRSAG stage vocabulary** (as reported by Alberta Transportation): planning, preliminary design,
detailed design, construction and pre-opening [EV-CA-005]. Provincially, the named stages are compressed into a
numbered ladder:

| TAC Canadian RSA Guide terminology | Alberta equivalent | Trigger (when conducted) |
|---|---|---|
| Planning Stage RSA | **Stage 1 RSA** | Once desired option selected in functional planning; also audit value-engineering proposals immediately after VE completes [EV-CA-007], [EV-CA-008] |
| Preliminary Design Stage RSA | **Stage 2 RSA** | At the 95% stage of the Functional Plan, before final submission; same trigger for bridge Design Data (DD) drawings [EV-CA-008] |
| Detailed Design Stage RSA | **Stage 3 RSA** | Prior to milestone "Modify Design Based on Recommendations from Others" (<75% project completion ≈ 90–95% design), giving final input before tender; traffic accommodation plans optionally audited here for complex detours/staging [EV-CA-008] |
| Pre-opening Stage RSA | **Pre-opening Stage RSA** | When works are substantially complete (markings/signing installed, budget remains to respond); **mandatory for green-field new-road construction**, considered for retrofits [EV-CA-008] |

Definitions of what each stage examines [EV-CA-007]:
- **Stage 1 (Planning)** — fundamental elements: alignment, laning, facility classification, access management
  concept, project limits, design speed, design criteria.
- **Stage 2 (Preliminary Design)** — still-changeable elements: cross-section dimensions,
  interchange/intersection layout, traffic operations, access, drainage, clear zones, road-user interaction.
- **Stage 3 (Detailed Design)** — final check just before submission of final design plans; closes out issues
  from earlier audits before construction.
- **Pre-opening** — implementation verification: signing location/visibility, markings/delineation, visual
  impacts, connectivity with existing infrastructure.

**2. In-service work sits outside the design-stage ladder**: TAC handles existing roads through the separate
*Canadian Guide to In-service Road Safety Reviews* (companion volume) [EV-CA-015]; Alberta explicitly declined
to adopt an in-service RSA stage [EV-CA-013]; the UNB model treats post-opening/existing as a fifth stage
[EV-CA-016].

**3. Ontario municipal vocabulary (Good Roads, 2023)**: feasibility → concept design → detailed design →
pre-opening/post-opening (post-opening variant ≤ ~3 months after opening) → existing conditions [EV-CA-019].

**Historical five-stage Canadian model (UNB 1999)**: feasibility (planning); draft (preliminary/layout) design
— completed before land acquisition is finalized; detailed design — upon plan completion, before contract
documents; pre-opening — day and night drive-through inspections, adverse weather where possible;
post-opening/existing [EV-CA-016].

Mapping to the MVP's stages 0–2 (proposal, not sourced fact): MVP Stage 0 ≈ Planning/Feasibility + Concept
(TAC Stage 1 / Good Roads feasibility+concept); MVP Stage 1 ≈ Preliminary→Detailed design (TAC Stages 2–3 /
Good Roads detailed design); MVP Stage 2 ≈ Pre-opening (+ existing-conditions mode). Final naming decision is
deferred to the geometry node.

## Inputs per stage

Commissioning-level inputs common to stages (Good Roads): the audit brief must give the team everything needed
for comprehensive assessment; background information includes known-but-unresolved safety issues, outstanding
deficiencies, design parameters/standards/guidelines adopted plus known departures, necessary trade-offs and
why, and community consultation input; the team audits the documents provided (drawings at minimum; optionally
traffic/speed data, planning studies, design reports, stakeholder correspondence, drone footage, crash history)
[EV-CA-021]. UNB similarly places "collection of background information" ahead of the commencement meeting
[EV-CA-017].

Stage-specific review scope (Alberta Appendix A outlines; the items listed are what the team expects to examine
and therefore the inputs it needs) [EV-CA-007]:

- **Stage 1 (Planning):** design criteria; design volume/traffic characteristics; route choice; project scope,
  function and traffic mix; access management and adjacent developments; continuity with existing network;
  environmental constraints.
- **Stage 2 (Preliminary Design):** typical cross-sections and variations; horizontal/vertical alignment
  details; visibility and sight distance; shoulder treatments, clear zones, roadside features; channelization;
  interchange/intersection spacing, layouts and traffic controls; traffic signals; vulnerable road users;
  driver expectancy; deviations from design domains; roadside hazards (barriers, guard rails, utility poles);
  future works and wider-network effects.
- **Stage 3 (Detailed Design):** actions taken as a result of previous audits; alignment details; signing and
  pavement markings placement/visibility; lighting (night visit) and drainage; bridges/culverts treatment; ties
  in with existing network; street furniture and landscaping.
- **Pre-opening:** results of design and design audits correctly implemented; implementation details — signing
  location and visibility, paint markings and delineation, visual impacts, connectivity with existing
  infrastructure [EV-CA-007].

For existing-conditions audits (Ontario), crash data may weight prioritization but should not be the sole risk
indicator; site examination is done on foot supplemented by drive-throughs, so no drawings input exists
[EV-CA-018], [EV-CA-019], [EV-CA-021].

## Outputs per stage

- **RSA report** — the universal output. Good Roads specifies minimum report contents: title/project
  introduction (road name, extent, locality, audit type/stage), background information (team composition with
  registration, client details, reason for audit, project plan marking finding locations, factual details and
  conditions of site visit(s), commencement/completion meeting records), findings tabulated by location and
  reference number including crash-type identification, risk assessment and recommendations, a formal
  concluding statement signed by the lead auditor, and appendices (key map, document list with dates/versions,
  photograph list) [EV-CA-021]. Alberta requires reports to follow TAC procedures, delivered within an agreed
  timeline of roughly 5 days (simple) to 20 days (complex), with a copy available to the department [EV-CA-011].
  Historically (UNB), the report *lists deficiencies* and "should not contain recommended remedial measures
  although exemplary solutions may be identified" [EV-CA-017].
- **Response report** — a formal designer/owner obligation across Canadian frameworks. Alberta enumerates
  exactly three responses: (1) accept in full and implement; (2) conditionally accept with documented reasons
  for modifications; (3) reject with reasons — and vests the appropriateness decision in the project owner, not
  the auditor [EV-CA-011]. BC requires the project team's detailed response to be placed in the project file,
  with copies of *both* report and response forwarded to the Ministry RSA Committee representative [EV-CA-014].
  UNB: the design team must give a documented response addressing all safety recommendations [EV-CA-017].
- **Records/filing** — Alberta's administration flowchart ends with "All documents filed for future retrieval",
  reflecting its legal-advice-driven documentation duties (maintain formal procedures, clear terms of reference,
  record of full documentation per audit) [EV-CA-012], [EV-CA-014].
- **Post-opening follow-up** — UNB includes a follow-up step after the completion meeting; monitoring of opened
  schemes exists in Canada only informally (no UK-style mandatory Stage 4 monitoring audit was found in any
  Canadian source reviewed) [EV-CA-017].

## Team and process requirements

- **Independence** is the core requirement: auditors independent of the design team and facility owner
  [EV-CA-026]; "fundamental requirement… aside from their competency" (Good Roads) [EV-CA-020]; Alberta
  requires demonstrated independence and credentials as proposal-selection criteria, with the RSA team retained
  as a paid sub-consultant of the prime consultant or directly by the department [EV-CA-010].
- **Size/composition:** minimum two people (Alberta "shall"; Good Roads team leader + ≥1 member, generally ≤4)
  [EV-CA-010], [EV-CA-020]; multidisciplinary expertise spanning road design, traffic operations, maintenance,
  construction and human factors [EV-CA-026]; specialist advisors (e.g., signal engineers, disability
  specialists) and key stakeholders may join without influencing findings [EV-CA-020].
- **Competence thresholds (most explicit = Alberta):** ≥2-day recognized RSA training course; ≥5 years relevant
  road safety/design/traffic engineering experience; ≥1 audit/year over the past two years; Team Leader: ≥5
  formal RSAs in 3 years incl. ≥3 design-stage; complex projects: ≥10 RSAs in 3 years incl. ≥5 leading
  [EV-CA-010]. Good Roads defines three qualification levels (lead/senior auditor, auditor, trainee; max two
  trainees) with training, accreditation, CPD and an Ontario auditor registry — noting "Road Safety Auditor is
  not a regulated profession in Ontario" [EV-CA-020].
- **Process shape:** commissioning phase (brief; team selection; background pack) → conducting phase
  (commencement meeting; all members independently review documents then reconcile findings cooperatively;
  site inspection by every member day + night where warranted, with photographic/video record and OH&S
  planning) → completion phase (report; completion meeting; response handling; follow-up) [EV-CA-017],
  [EV-CA-020], [EV-CA-021]. Desktop-only audits are non-compliant unless justified and documented [EV-CA-020].
- **Legal posture:** Alberta sought its own liability advice per the TAC Guide's lead and mandates documented
  procedures, terms of reference per audit, awareness of responsibilities, competent teams, and full records
  [EV-CA-003], [EV-CA-012].
- **Alternate delivery (P3/design-build):** the proponent hires the RSA team (team identified in the EOI and
  locked after short-listing except by owner approval), RFPs require use of the AT Guidelines + TAC CRSAG, and
  owners may run Stages 1–2 on the conceptual design pre-RFP and issue the report/response with the RFP to cap
  change-order exposure [EV-CA-009 context; Alberta Appendix B — see artifact URL in EV-CA-009 record].
- **Project-type scoping:** Alberta applies a matrix of recommended stages by project type/location
  (interchanges, widening, intersection improvements, ring roads, reconstruction) and requires RSAs on
  federally funded projects regardless of type [EV-CA-009].

## Risk/severity semantics used in Canadian practice

- Traditional Canadian practice (TAC lineage, echoed by Alberta and BC definitions) is **qualitative**: audits
  identify safety issues and make suggestions; there is no mandated numeric risk score visible in any accessed
  Canadian policy text [EV-CA-003], [EV-CA-004], [EV-CA-012]. Whether the paywalled CRSAG itself prescribes a
  severity scale is **Unknown**.
- Ontario (Good Roads) introduces the first explicit machinery found: a **risk assessment framework** plus a
  **severity guidance matrix** (Figures 3–4) applied to each finding, combining likelihood and consequence with
  severity guided by speed environment and traffic volume; findings are recorded with crash types and
  risk-assessed recommendations [EV-CA-021]. This imports Austroads-style semantics into Canadian municipal use
  [EV-CA-018].
- Alberta's stage-issue table (Table A-1) organizes potential issues by stage but assigns no severity tiers in
  the accessible text [EV-CA-007].
- Existing-roads risk prioritization (Ontario): order candidate roads by risk proxies — traffic volume and
  speed limit (or operating speed), plus usage-change events (e.g., new bike lane); crash history used only as
  a weighting due to regression-to-the-mean [EV-CA-018 context; see EV-CA-019/EV-CA-021 records for the
  guideline source].

## Differences from UK and US practice

- **Definition wording.** TAC (as quoted provincially): "formal and independent safety performance review…
  by an experienced team of safety specialists" [EV-CA-003]. US/FHWA: "formal safety performance examination…
  by an independent, multidisciplinary team that qualitatively estimates…" [EV-CA-025]. Same family, different
  emphasis (specialist-experience vs multi-disciplinary-qualitative).
- **Mandatory vs voluntary.** No national Canadian mandate exists; TAC material is guidance that provinces
  adopt internally (BC circular: projects "shall be subject to" the RSA process [EV-CA-014]; Alberta: same, via
  its business plan [EV-CA-012]). The UK's GG 119 is a requirements-bearing standard for the trunk road and
  motorway network [EV-CA-023]; the US approach is federally encouraged but not mandated, usable "in any phase…
  and on projects of any size" [EV-CA-025].
- **Stage architecture.** Canada: named lifecycle stages, with provinces renumbering them (Alberta Stage 1–3 +
  pre-opening) [EV-CA-005], [EV-CA-006]. UK: fixed numbered Stages 1–4, where Stage 3 = completion of
  construction and Stage 4 = monitoring [EV-CA-024] — note the **numbering collision** (UK Stage 3 ≠ Alberta
  Stage 3). US: no numbered stages; phase-named prompt lists (planning, preliminary design, final design, work
  zone, pre-opening, existing road) around an eight-step process [EV-CA-025].
- **Existing roads.** Canada splits the concern: design-stage RSAs vs a dedicated in-service *reviews* guide
  [EV-CA-015], with Alberta formally rejecting in-service RSAs [EV-CA-013]; the UK folds monitoring into Stage
  4 [EV-CA-024]; the FHWA treats existing-road audits as a normal RSA type [EV-CA-025].
- **Auditor credentialing.** UK: Certificate of Competency regime under GG 119 [EV-CA-023]. Canada: no national
  register; Alberta uses quantified experience quotas and pre-qualified consultant lists [EV-CA-010]; Ontario
  is building a voluntary registry through an association, explicitly "not a regulated profession" [EV-CA-020].
- **Response mechanics.** All three jurisdictions require a formal response; Canada's distinctive contribution
  (Alberta) is a codified accept / conditional-accept / reject taxonomy with owner-side accountability
  [EV-CA-011], paralleling the FHWA steps 7–8 owned by the design team/owner [EV-CA-025].
- **Recommendation authority.** Older Canadian synthesis (UNB) keeps remedial design out of the auditor's pen
  ("describe the problem"), while newer Ontario/Austroads-derived practice pairs each finding with assessed,
  specific recommendations [EV-CA-017] vs [EV-CA-021] — a live intra-Canadian spread worth honoring in product
  copy.

## Conflicts and gaps

1. **Stage-number collision across jurisdictions** (Alberta Stage 3 = detailed design; UK Stage 3 =
   post-construction). The app must namespace stage IDs per jurisdiction [EV-CA-006] vs [EV-CA-024].
2. **Granularity inconsistency inside Canada**: TAC's narrative list includes a "construction" stage
   [EV-CA-005]; Alberta operationalizes construction via optional traffic-accommodation-plan audits plus
   pre-opening [EV-CA-008]; UNB adds post-opening as a stage [EV-CA-016]; Good Roads splits pre-/post-opening
   and existing conditions [EV-CA-019]. No single canonical Canadian ladder exists.
3. **CRSAG full text inaccessible (paywalled)** — internal definitions, prompt-list structure and any severity
   scheme cannot be verified beyond provincial quotations and catalogue metadata [EV-CA-001]. Recorded as
   inaccessible per ticket instructions.
4. **Currency risk**: the two strongest provincial instruments found (BC T-02/04; Alberta Guidelines) both date
   to March 2004; no superseding or amended versions were located in this pass — currency Unknown.
5. **Ontario ministry-level guidance absent**: no MTO-published RSA standard surfaced; the operative Ontario
   document is an association (Good Roads) guideline for municipal roads [EV-CA-018].
6. **Alberta Table 2 cell values unrecoverable** from text extraction (checkmark positions lost); only the
   existence of the project-type matrix and the federal-funding requirement are citable [EV-CA-009].
7. **Risk scoring is not standardized nationally** — qualitative tradition vs Ontario's Austroads-style matrix
   [EV-CA-012] vs [EV-CA-021].
