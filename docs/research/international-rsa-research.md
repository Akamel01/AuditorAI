## Is there a true international RSA standard?

**Direct answer: No. No single authoritative *international standard* for road safety audit exists. What exists internationally is guidance, recommendations and complementary methodologies — chiefly PIARC's technical reports — plus regional law (EU) and national standards.** Reasoning:

1. **Standards bodies do not publish one.** ISO's road traffic safety output is organizational management systems (ISO 39001) and commuting safety good practice (ISO 39002); no ISO standard for project-stage RSA of infrastructure was identified [EV-IN-006][EV-IN-007][EV-IN-008]. ISO 39001 is additionally mid-revision (stage 90.92, ISO/WD 39001.2 in development), making it unstable as a citable anchor [EV-IN-007].
2. **The nearest international instrument is PIARC guidance, and PIARC itself frames it as non-binding.** PIARC 2023R40EN is titled a *technical report* / *guidelines* [EV-IN-001], and the PIARC Road Safety Manual explicitly states that adoption of the four check types "is up to individual countries" [EV-IN-003]. PIARC is a knowledge-exchange association (125 member governments, UN ECOSOC consultative status), not a treaty body or standards body.
3. **Where RSAs are mandatory, the mandate is jurisdictional, not international.** The only binding regime found is the EU Directive for the trans-European network and EU-funded roads [EV-IN-013]; national mandates (UK, Australia, etc.) live inside the UK/USA/Canada/UAE packs this product already treats separately.
4. **UN/WHO machinery recommends but does not prescribe.** The UN Global Plan urges audits on all new roads with independent accredited experts and a 3-star floor [EV-IN-010], and WHO merely monitors whether countries report having such practice [EV-IN-011][EV-IN-012]. These are voluntary political commitments with KPI language, not auditable procedure clauses.
5. **iRAP positions itself as complementary.** iRAP says Star Rating assessments can be performed "in conjunction with" RSAs and strengthen them (e.g., SR4D); it does not claim to be an audit standard [EV-IN-009][EV-IN-014].

Therefore the product must NOT present "International" as compliance with a named binding standard. It should ship as an explicitly qualified baseline synthesized from the named sources above.

## What 'International' can mean for this product

Three defensible constructions, in order of recommendation:

**Option A — Qualified synthesized baseline (recommended).** Label the mode "International — synthesized baseline" and pin each element of the app's workflow/checklists/report format to a named source: PIARC 2023R40EN for process skeleton (commissioning / undertaking / completion), independence rules, and stage prompt lists [EV-IN-001][EV-IN-004]; PIARC Road Safety Manual for definitions and the RISM context [EV-IN-002][EV-IN-003]; FHWA/Austroads/UK materials only inside their jurisdiction packs, not in the international baseline [EV-IN-009]. Trade-offs: highest defensibility and honesty; slightly more UI copy burden (attribution everywhere); requires a maintenance rule for when PIARC updates.

**Option B — PIARC-implementation framing.** Present the International mode narrowly as "implements the PIARC Road Safety Audit process (2023R40EN)". Trade-offs: simplest citation story and very defensible; but overstates fidelity unless every checklist item genuinely traces to that single document, and it discards the useful Safe-System/global-target overlay.

**Option C — Global-targets alignment overlay.** Frame International mode around UN Global Plan KPIs (audits pre-feasibility→detailed design; independent accredited experts; 3-star floor) plus optional iRAP SR4D outputs [EV-IN-009][EV-IN-010]. Trade-offs: strong policy narrative and funder resonance; weakest procedural specificity — these are goals, not a method, so it cannot stand alone and would need Option A/B underneath.

Recommended combination: **A with B as its spine**, offering C as an optional reporting layer. Any wording implying certification, conformity, or "compliance with the international standard" is unsupported by the evidence and must be avoided.

## Stage concepts in international material

Stage/lifecycle language found (all quoted or closely paraphrased from fetched official text):

- PIARC 2023 guide provides audit prompt lists "for a RSA at various stages of project development", and the Road Safety Manual enumerates its checklists at **feasibility study, preliminary design, detailed design, and pre- and post-traffic opening**; the audit process itself has three parts: **commissioning, undertaking, completion** [EV-IN-004].
- PIARC's definition spans "all stages, from planning to early operation" [EV-IN-002].
- The four check types differ mainly by timing/scope across the scheme lifecycle — impact assessment at initial planning, audit through design and construction, inspection on existing roads, assessment programmes on networks [EV-IN-003].
- EU Directive (as summarised by PIARC): audits at **draft design, detailed design, pre-opening and early operation**; some jurisdictions add feasibility-stage audits (Australia, NZ, Ireland, Qatar, South Africa) and/or during-works audits (USA, Qatar, South Africa) [EV-IN-013].
- UN Global Plan KPI: audits on new roads "**pre-feasibility through to detailed design**" [EV-IN-010].
- Note: PIARC cautions that safety inspection of existing roads is sometimes called an "audit" in some countries — terminology drifts by jurisdiction [EV-IN-003].

Mapping to the product's stages 0–2 (proposal, clearly our inference, NOT source language): Stage 0 ≈ feasibility/planning (impact-assessment era), Stage 1 ≈ preliminary + detailed design, Stage 2 ≈ pre-opening/post-opening (early operation). This mapping must be validated against tickets G2/G4 before any user-facing label uses source vocabulary.

## Conflicts and gaps

- **No global mandatory layer.** Guidance (PIARC) vs. regional law (EU) vs. national mandates vs. voluntary UN KPIs coexist; none is worldwide-binding. Any "International = required by X" claim fails [EV-IN-001][EV-IN-010][EV-IN-013].
- **Terminology inconsistency.** Audit vs. inspection vs. impact assessment boundaries vary by country; PIARC notes inspection-of-existing-roads is sometimes called an audit [EV-IN-003]. Product glossary must define terms once and stick to them.
- **Stage taxonomies disagree.** PIARC lists five checkpoint moments incl. feasibility; the EU lists four without feasibility; several countries add feasibility and/or during-works stages [EV-IN-004][EV-IN-013]. A single fixed stage set cannot claim universal authority.
- **ISO mismatch.** ISO 39001 governs organizations' road traffic safety management, not infrastructure projects, and is pending revision [EV-IN-006][EV-IN-007]; citing it as the basis of an audit workflow would misrepresent the source.
- **Absence is hard to prove.** The claim that no ISO RSA standard exists rests on catalogue searches on 2026-08-21, marked medium confidence [EV-IN-008]; a definitive statement would require a full ISO catalogue sweep or TC 241 confirmation.
- **Access limitations recorded.** PIARC 2023R40EN PDF could not be machine-parsed this session (details verified via official metadata page and the Road Safety Manual instead); ISO full texts paywalled. No clause numbers or verbatim quotes from inside those documents appear anywhere in this artifact — everything traces to what was actually readable.
