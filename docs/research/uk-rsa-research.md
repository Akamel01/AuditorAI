The Standards for Highways platform is jointly branded for all four Overseeing Organisations
(National Highways, Transport Scotland, Welsh Government, DfI Northern Ireland) and serves
country-specific selections of the DMRB [EV-UK-019].

## Stage model

**Native UK stage names (authoritative, GG 119 v2.0.1 clause 1.3)** [EV-UK-002]:

1. **Stage 1 — Completion of preliminary design.** Trigger: preliminary design complete, before
   publication of draft orders; often the last chance to change land requirements; should address
   matters bearing on land take, licence or easement; may be combined with Stage 2 if no separate
   preliminary design exists [EV-UK-008].
2. **Stage 2 — Completion of detailed design.** Trigger: detailed design complete; focuses on
   detailed aspects; must review Stage 1 response-report actions and reiterate incomplete ones
   [EV-UK-009].
3. **Stage 3 — Completion of construction.** Trigger: construction complete; recommended before
   opening; must occur within 1 month of opening unless otherwise agreed; daylight **and darkness**
   site visits with police and maintaining-agent invitees [EV-UK-010].
4. **Stage 4 — Post-opening monitoring.** Trigger: 12 months of *validated* post-opening collision
   data available; evidence-led collision analysis; report only if collisions occurred; no response
   report [EV-UK-011].

### Does a UK "Stage 0" exist?

**No.** The authoritative answer is negative:

- Clause 1.3 enumerates exactly four stages and requires RSA "at each of the following stages";
  a full-text search of the official v2.0.1 document finds **no** "Stage 0", "Stage F", or
  feasibility-stage audit anywhere [EV-UK-004].
- The standard's own definition of road safety audit ties the activity to the four completion
  points above [EV-UK-003].
- The closest sanctioned construct is the **interim RSA**: applicable to the whole or part of a
  scheme at *any time* during design or construction, explicitly **neither mandatory nor a
  substitute** for Stages 1–4, decided case-by-case by the Overseeing Organisation, and following
  the process of the relevant stage [EV-UK-005].
- Independent professional-body commentary (SoRSA/CIHT) confirms the UK national standard has four
  stages and attributes "Stage F — Feasibility" to *other* countries' practice [EV-UK-022].

**Product implication:** a canonical "Stage 0" in the MVP cannot be labelled a UK native stage.
The honest UK mapping is: canonical pre-design stage → either "no UK equivalent" or an optional
**Interim RSA**, clearly qualified as such.

## Inputs per stage

The RSA brief defines the audit scope and is stage-specific; the design organisation prepares it
for Stages 1–3 and the Overseeing Organisation approves and issues it (Overseeing Organisation
prepares it for Stage 4) [EV-UK-014]. Appendix C of GG 119 itemises brief contents:

- Scheme description/objective, extents, programme; design standards applied; design speeds and
  speed limits; existing and forecast traffic flows/queues [EV-UK-014]
- Pedestrian/cyclist/equestrian desire lines (with GG 142 walking, cycling and horse-riding
  assessment references) [EV-UK-014]
- Environmental constraints and locality description [EV-UK-014]
- **Collision data analysis: most recent 36 months minimum for Stages 1–3; 12 months of
  validated post-opening data for Stage 4** [EV-UK-011], [EV-UK-014]
- Departures from standards with approval status; previous RSA reports, response reports and
  evidence of agreed actions; strategic decisions excluded from audit influence; drawing/document
  register [EV-UK-014], [EV-UK-015]
- Police and maintaining agent contact details (required for Stage 3) [EV-UK-010], [EV-UK-014]

Per-stage emphasis is supported by Appendix B checklists (local alignment, visibility,
new/existing road interface, etc., staged across Stages 1–3) [EV-UK-002]; Stage 4 inputs are the
validated collision dataset plus operational data where available, and layout-change history
[EV-UK-011].

## Outputs per stage

- **RSA report (Stages 1–4):** written report by the RSA team issued *directly* to the Overseeing
  Organisation; contains unique document reference, scheme description, brief/team approvals,
  membership, site-visit details, marked-up location plan, signed team statement ("We certify that
  this road safety audit has been carried out in accordance with GG 119"), and information list;
  separate templates apply to Stages 1–3 (Appendix D) and Stage 4 (Appendix E) [EV-UK-014],
  [EV-UK-015].
- **Problem/recommendation structure:** each problem = location + summary + likely collision
  types/injuries; each followed by one proportionate, viable recommendation; "consider"/"must"
  forbidden; "monitor" recommendations heavily constrained [EV-UK-015].
- **RSA response report (Stages 1–3 only; none for Stage 4):** produced by the design organisation
  in collaboration with the Overseeing Organisation; includes a **decision log** reiterating every
  problem/recommendation with the design organisation's response (accept / accept-with-alternative /
  disagree-with-reasons), the Overseeing Organisation's response, and the agreed action; signed by
  both; finalisation within one month is recommended; kept on the scheme file [EV-UK-016].
- **Exemption file note (Appendix A):** the Overseeing Organisation's recorded justification where
  RSA is not applied; expressly *not* a substitute for a departure from standard [EV-UK-006].
- **Repetition:** repeated audits cover only changed elements; Stage 1/2 audits expire after
  5 years [EV-UK-018].

**Exceptions procedure note:** GG 119 v2.0.1 has **no "exception report"**. The legacy HD 19-era
exception-report concept survives only in older industry literature; the current instrument is the
response report + decision log [EV-UK-016], with exemptions from auditing handled by the exemption
file note [EV-UK-006].

## Team and process requirements

- Composition: team leader + at least one team member; observers allowed up to two (not part of
  team); specialist advisors (e.g. complex signals, smart motorway technology) approved by and
  independent of the team [EV-UK-012].
- Independence: the team shall be independent of scheme conception, design, construction and
  operation; individuals may come from different organisations including the Overseeing
  Organisation's and design organisation's staff, provided independence holds [EV-UK-012].
- Approval: the Overseeing Organisation approves the proposed team before the audit; approvals are
  scheme- and stage-specific; competency demonstrated via RSA-specific CV with training, CPD and
  experience [EV-UK-012], [EV-UK-013].
- Competence floors: 10 days formal training; ≥2 days CPD in the last 12 months; 1 yr / 2 yr /
  4 yr experience (observer/member/leader paths per Table 3.8.2); 5 RSAs in 24 months (member) or
  12 months (leader); at least one member must hold a **certificate of competency** (two routes:
  portfolio of evidence or training course; provider independent of employer; certificate has no
  expiry) [EV-UK-013].
- Communication: methods agreed to preserve independence; communications should be recorded
  [EV-UK-014].
- Site visits: whole team together, maximum six attendees; Stage 1/2 daytime; Stage 3 daytime +
  darkness with police and maintaining agent invited; Stage 4 visits only on specified
  collision-data triggers [EV-UK-010], [EV-UK-011], [EV-UK-017].

## Risk/severity semantics used in UK practice

- **No numeric severity or likelihood scoring exists in GG 119.** Findings are framed qualitatively:
  each problem must identify the *potential collision type(s)* or road-user injuries likely to
  result, at a described location [EV-UK-015]. (Verified by full-text review; the standard contains
  no risk-rating matrix.)
- Recommendations must be "proportionate and viable" and may include commissioning further work by
  the design organisation [EV-UK-015].
- Formal risk assessment sits **outside the audit**: GG 119 assumes identified RSA actions will be
  subjected to formal design-organisation risk assessments before implementation [EV-UK-024].
- "Strategic decisions" (route choice, junction type, approved departures) are declared in the brief
  and effectively ring-fenced from audit challenge [EV-UK-015].
- Departures from geometric standards follow separate processes (Transport Scotland Departures
  Guide [EV-UK-020]; Welsh Government Chief Highway Engineer endorsement via Technical Review Panel
  for active travel guidance [EV-UK-021]) — distinct from the RSA finding-response flow.
- Product caution: importing severity scores into a "UK mode" would be an extension beyond GG 119
  and should be surfaced as such, not presented as UK-native semantics.

## Conflicts and gaps

1. **Scope boundary vs product ambition.** GG 119 is mandatory for motorways and all-purpose trunk
   roads (the strategic networks of the four Overseeing Organisations) [EV-UK-001], [EV-UK-006];
   application to local roads is at each authority's discretion. Widely-cited claims that GG 119 is
   the de facto default for local highway authorities appear in consultant marketing material but
   were **not verified against an authoritative source** — treat as Unknown.
2. **Legacy terminology drift.** Pre-2019 HD 19-era artefacts ("exception report", "designer's
   response") still circulate in industry literature; GG 119 replaced them with the response report
   and decision log [EV-UK-001], [EV-UK-016]. The app's vocabulary should follow v2.0.1 terms.
3. **"Stage 0" pressure.** Feasibility-level reviews are marketed by practitioners and used abroad
   (Stage F), but have no basis in UK national guidance [EV-UK-004], [EV-UK-022]; any UK "Stage 0"
   feature must be labelled as an extension (or as an interim RSA) to avoid false harmonisation.
4. **v2.0.1 vs Rev 2 circulation.** Many mirrors host Revision 2 (Jan 2020); v2.0.1 (Apr 2025) made
   "incremental updates resulting from the cyclical document review" [EV-UK-001], including renamed
   Overseeing Organisation specialist contacts (e.g. Transport Scotland: Roads Directorate instead
   of Head of Standards) [EV-UK-019]. A public clause-by-clause diff is not available.
5. **Devolved divergence is thin but real.** Scotland and Wales apply the same DMRB/GG 119 with
   supplementary instruments (TSIAs, Welsh annexes/PAGs) rather than alternative audit regimes
   [EV-UK-020], [EV-UK-021]; no conflicting Scottish/Welsh/NI stage models were found.
