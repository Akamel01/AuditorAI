# USA Road Safety Audit Research (FHWA / AASHTO / NCHRP)

Research artifact answering Wayfinder ticket R3 (`workflow/wayfinder/maps/mvp/tickets/R3-usa-fhwa-rsa.md`).
Retrieved 2026-08-21. Sources are FHWA (.gov) program pages and publications plus NCHRP/TRB syntheses.
Blogs and Wikipedia were used only for orientation; no normative claim below rests on them.

**Headline answer:** US federal guidance does NOT define formal numbered audit stages comparable to the UK's Stage 1/2/3/4. It names audit points by **project phase** (planning, preliminary design, final design, work zone, pre-opening, existing road/operational) attached to a **single, phase-independent eight-step audit process**. The only numbers involved are the numbers of the **prompt lists** (1-7) in the 2006 Guidelines, which are checklist identifiers, not stage numbers. US practice must not be mapped onto UK stage numbers.

## Stage model

- Federal guidance assigns audits to **named project phases**, not numbered stages. The 2006 Guidelines' structure is: pre-construction (Planning Stage Audit, Preliminary Design Stage Audit, Final Design Stage Audit), construction (Work Zone Traffic Control Plan Audit, Pre-Opening Stage Audit), post-construction (Existing Road Audit), plus a Land Use Development Proposals Audit for development projects [EV-US-006].
- The integers 1-7 in the Guidelines are **prompt-list numbers**, i.e., checklist sequence identifiers; nowhere does federal guidance label these "Stage 1 ... Stage 7" [EV-US-006].
- FHWA case-study guidance groups the same phases into three macro-types: pre-construction (planning/feasibility and design), construction (work zone, mid-construction design changes, and pre-opening before the road carries traffic), and post-construction/operational (existing roads, usually triggered by a demonstrated crash problem) [EV-US-015].
- The Model RSA Policy phrases the same options as: prior to construction during the planning stage; prior to construction during the design stage; during construction including the work zone; at any time a facility is in operation [EV-US-008].
- FHWA's current messaging avoids fixed checkpoints: RSAs fit "any phase of project development" on "any sized project" [EV-US-002], and agencies are encouraged to audit "at the earliest stage possible," while alternatives are still open [EV-US-005].
- One eight-step process applies regardless of the phase audited; the phase changes the prompt list and inputs, not the process skeleton [EV-US-003], [EV-US-006], [EV-US-015].

Implication for the MVP: model USA audits as `(project_phase, single_process)` rather than numbered stages. Feasible US phase labels grounded in sources: planning, preliminary design, final design, work zone/construction, pre-opening, operational/existing road, land-use development proposal.

## Process requirements

The documented federal process is the **eight-step RSA model**, consistent across the 2006 Guidelines, the RSDP tool summary, the Steps-of-Conduct page, and the 2021 countermeasure sheet [EV-US-003], [EV-US-004]:

1. Identify project or existing road to be audited - owner/design team [EV-US-003], [EV-US-004]
2. Select independent, multidisciplinary RSA team - owner/design team [EV-US-004], [EV-US-009], [EV-US-010]
3. Start-up / pre-audit meeting to review project information - RSA team, with owner and designer participating [EV-US-003], [EV-US-011]
4. Perform field reviews under various conditions - RSA team [EV-US-003], [EV-US-004]
5. Analyze findings and prepare the report - RSA team [EV-US-003], [EV-US-004]
6. Present findings to the Project Owner/Design Team (preliminary findings meeting) - RSA team [EV-US-004], [EV-US-011]
7. Prepare formal response - design team/project owner [EV-US-004], [EV-US-012]
8. Incorporate findings into the project when appropriate - design team/project owner [EV-US-003], [EV-US-004]

Supporting process facts:

- FHWA also publishes a simplified three-role view: the public agency identifies the subject and the team, the team performs the audit and reports, and the agency follows up on the findings [EV-US-003].
- Field review is a defined element of every RSA in the Model Policy's elements list, alongside the start-up meeting, issue identification, suggestions, preliminary findings meeting, and written report [EV-US-011].
- The owner is expected to supply materials, answer questions during the site visit and analysis, and circulate the final report; the team identifies what information it needs [EV-US-011].
- Draft-report handling: the team may issue a draft for comment; owner comments should correct facts or advise on feasibility, not change findings, and a roughly one-week turnaround is suggested [EV-US-011].

## Inputs per phase

Verified at the federal level:

- The owner supplies "necessary materials" and the team specifies the information it needs; content is reviewed at the start-up meeting against project information [EV-US-003], [EV-US-011].
- For existing-road (post-construction) audits, crash history is expected to be available and is reviewed so that known crash patterns are addressed; issue identification itself draws on crash data analysis and/or operational, geometric, and human factors review [EV-US-011].
- The FLH Toolkit supplies structured worksheets for requesting assistance, scheduling, data analysis, field reviews, and documenting issues and suggestions, i.e., the de facto input templates for resource-constrained agencies [EV-US-020].
- Phase-specific prompt lists (planning vs preliminary design vs final design vs work zone vs pre-opening vs existing road vs land-use development) are the Guideline's mechanism for tailoring inputs to phase [EV-US-006], [EV-US-015].

Unknown (recorded honestly): the itemized per-phase input lists (which drawings, traffic data, and background documents belong in the audit brief for each phase) live in Chapters 5-7 of FHWA-SA-06-06. The PDF could not be text-extracted in this pass (binary fetch), so those itemized lists are **not verified verbatim** and must not be quoted in the app until checked against the manual PDF. Do not invent them.

## Outputs per phase

Phase-independent outputs, per federal sources:

- A formal RSA report is always produced; this, plus the formal response, is what separates an RSA from a traditional safety review [EV-US-013].
- Report anatomy (from FHWA tribal case studies, representative of FHWA practice): key findings and suggestions summarized per issue, typically one to two pages each - a one-sentence issue statement at the top, description with photographs of how the condition contributes to crashes, a prioritization rating from the risk matrix, and suggested ways to address the issue [EV-US-017].
- Preliminary findings are presented orally to the owner and design team before the report is finalized [EV-US-011].
- The final written report goes to the road owner, who circulates it to stakeholders and the RSA coordinator [EV-US-011].
- The owner's formal written response is the closure document: agreement or disagreement with each finding, actions proposed, and justification where no action is taken [EV-US-012]. In the SC DOT program documented by FHWA, the district administrator had 45 days to respond in writing [EV-US-022].
- Findings are then incorporated into the project where appropriate (Step 8) [EV-US-004].

No federal source was found prescribing different report structures per project phase; the report format is stable across phases, with phase affecting content emphasis via the prompt lists [EV-US-006].

## Team and process requirements

- Independence: team members should have had no previous involvement with the project or facility audited; permissible sourcing includes uninvolved staff from other divisions, staff of other agencies under reciprocal arrangements, private-sector consultants, and specialist contributors (maintenance, enforcement, emergency services) [EV-US-009]. FHWA's overview frames the whole method as performed by a team "independent of the project" [EV-US-001], [EV-US-013].
- Multidisciplinary composition: multiple disciplines spanning safety, law enforcement, operations and/or maintenance, considering all road users [EV-US-010]; FHWA-SA-14-003's case teams typically mixed engineering, planning, and enforcement staff [EV-US-015].
- Human factors: accounting for road-user capabilities and limitations is an essential element of an RSA and distinguishes it from traditional reviews [EV-US-013].
- Division of duties: the audit team owns the examination and reporting (steps 3-6); the owner/design team owns selection, response, and incorporation (steps 1, 2, 7, 8) [EV-US-004].
- Unknown: a specific minimum team size or certification requirement is not established in any federal source verified this pass; treat any team-size number as agency-specific until verified against FHWA-SA-06-06 Section 4.2 in the manual PDF.

## Risk/severity semantics used in US practice

- US federal RSA methodology is explicitly **qualitative**: the RSA "qualitatively estimates and reports on potential road safety issues" [EV-US-001].
- Where prioritization is wanted, FHWA guidance combines **expected crash frequency** x **crash severity** in a matrix, fed by historical crash data, team expert judgment, or both [EV-US-016].
- A worked federal example rates each issue qualitatively for frequency (exposure x probability) and severity (speeds, collision types, vulnerable-user exposure), combines them, and assigns letter grades A-F from lowest to highest priority, stressing that the result reflects team judgment, not measurement [EV-US-017].
- No federal requirement for a numeric probability-by-severity scoring scheme (UK/Austroads style) was found in the verified sources; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- Quantitative figures appear only as program-evidence claims, e.g., the 10-60 percent total-crash-reduction benefit attributed to evaluations [EV-US-014], and as optional decision-support tools (IHSDM, 3-D visualization) in case studies [EV-US-015].

Design consequence: the app's USA mode should support optional qualitative frequency/severity tagging (including the A-F convention) but must not present any numeric risk score as federally mandated.

## Conflicts and gaps

- Prompt-list numbering invites stage confusion: the Guidelines number their prompt lists 1-7 [EV-US-006], which third parties sometimes misread as numbered stages. Federal text names stages, never numbers them.
- Terminology drift: "road safety audit" vs "road safety assessment" [EV-US-019], and FHWA explicitly tolerates local renaming (assessments, evaluations, safety impact teams) [EV-US-021]. Legacy literature also uses "road safety audit review" for existing-road audits [EV-US-018].
- Vintage gap: the foundational guideline is from February 2006 [EV-US-007]. Current FHWA pages (updated 2015 and 2025) restate the same eight steps rather than replacing the document [EV-US-003], [EV-US-001]; no superseding federal edition was found, but none was explicitly ruled out either.
- Voluntariness: the Model Policy states it is not a Federal requirement [EV-US-008], and no verified federal source makes RSAs mandatory for any project class; obligations appear only where agencies adopt them (e.g., the Model Policy's threshold options) [EV-US-008].
- Evidence quality: the 10-60 percent benefit range aggregates heterogeneous evaluations [EV-US-014]; it is a marketing-grade statistic, not a per-phase performance guarantee.
- Verification gap: per-phase input checklists (Guidelines Chs. 5-7) and team-selection details (Sec. 4.2) remain unread from the primary PDF this pass; everything asserted about them above is limited to structure visible on the official landing page [EV-US-006], [EV-US-007].
